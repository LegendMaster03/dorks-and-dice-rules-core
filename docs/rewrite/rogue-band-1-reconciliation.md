# Rogue — Band 1 reconciliation (working decisions)

**Status:** Partial owner-approved design record, 2026-10-23. **Not implemented as a native class.** Scope: Rogue class levels 1–20, no subclasses, prestige classes or executable runtime.

## Approved: Sneak Attack frequency

**Owner decision (2026-10-23):** Sneak Attack applies to **every successful qualifying attack**, not only once per turn or round. The 5.x once-per-turn cap is removed for native Dorks & Dice. The historical 3.x repeated-attack capability is preserved.

- Determine eligibility **separately for each attack at the time it resolves**; an earlier qualifying hit does not automatically qualify later hits.
- When the Rogue's attack reveals their location or otherwise ends a hidden/unseen advantage under the applicable native stealth rules, subsequent attacks must qualify by another valid condition or regain eligibility legitimately.
- **Surprise is not a blanket Sneak Attack prerequisite.** Detection mechanics also apply during ongoing combat. Other established vulnerabilities may persist even after the Rogue is detected; the qualifying details remain separately open.
- Each eligible successful attack receives **one** application of the Rogue's class-level Sneak Attack damage. A single attack does not receive multiple duplicate Sneak Attack dice pools merely because several qualifications apply.
- This feature **does not grant additional attacks**, remove action/resource restrictions, or resolve the system-wide BAB/Extra Attack progression.
- The design deliberately allows a character with legitimately acquired additional attacks and maintained Sneak Attack eligibility to deal Sneak Attack damage on each such hit, even if that produces substantially higher damage than a simple single-attack turn.

**Rationale:** Basic play remains straightforward; interested players may obtain greater combat effectiveness through tactical preparation, maintaining favorable positions or other legitimate qualifying attack opportunities, without a hard per-turn damage cap.

## Approved: Sneak Attack eligibility principle

**Owner-approved native rule (2026-10-23), exact wording:**

> A Rogue can apply Sneak Attack damage to a successful attack when the target has failed to detect or anticipate the attack, or when the target has an explicitly established vulnerability that the Rogue can exploit with a precision strike.
>
> Detection uses the shared awareness and detection mechanics. Other qualifying vulnerabilities are established by specific conditions, actions, features, or successful techniques.
>
> Advantage does not independently establish Sneak Attack eligibility. Each attack is evaluated separately.

**Interpretation and boundary:** This builds upon historical Rogue precision damage while making failed detection or anticipation independently relevant. The shared surprise-awareness procedures can inform the determination, but a formal surprise round is not required. The target can know the Rogue's location and still have an established vulnerability. An Advantage roll by itself, flanking by name, or the presence of an ally is not sufficient without an explicitly established qualifying rule. A successful qualifying attack can activate the separately approved Sneak Attack damage frequency once, even when multiple routes qualify.

**Not yet approved:** An exhaustive list of eligible conditions, actions or techniques; the timing/duration of anticipation and awareness; whether and how an attack reveals its attacker; qualification through existing flanking or denied-Dexterity defenses; unusual attack forms or explicit precision-damage immunities; exact interactions with Cunning Strike, concealment/visibility, critical damage dice and general combat action rules. The fixed 30-foot cap is removed. Sneak Attack with a weapon requires proficiency with that weapon; neither Finesse nor Ranged properties are mandatory. Neither Disadvantage nor Weapon Mastery is imposed as an additional Sneak Attack weapon-eligibility rule. Normal attack range and visibility still apply. Swashbuckler's eventual place among classes, subclasses and prestige classes remains open, rather than a reason to narrow the Rogue's base feature.

## Approved: range, weapons, damage immunity and distinct defensive grants

**Owner-approved rulings (2026-10-23):**

- **No fixed Sneak Attack distance cap.** An attack from long range can qualify, including a sniper's attack. Ordinary weapon/effect range, targeting, visibility and the established detection-or-vulnerability criteria still apply; remove the historical 30-foot Sneak Attack range limit.
- **Weapon proficiency is the Sneak Attack weapon requirement.** A Rogue can apply Sneak Attack with **any weapon they are proficient with**, if the attack hits and satisfies the independently approved detection-or-vulnerability requirements. Finesse and Ranged weapons remain familiar Rogue choices, but those properties are **not required**. Proficient longsword, greatsword or other unconventional weapon builds are valid without a special Sneak Attack Disadvantage penalty or reduction in Sneak Attack dice. Ordinary attack modifiers (including Disadvantage when independently imposed) still apply normally. **Weapon Mastery gives only its normal benefits** and neither replaces proficiency nor separately grants Sneak Attack eligibility. Proficiency is earned through normal class, species, feat, multiclass or other legitimate sources; no blanket Rogue proficiency with martial weapons is implied. The treatment of spell attacks, unarmed strikes and other non-weapon attacks awaits the general attack-category rules.
- **Immunity is immunity; creature type or anatomy alone is not immunity.** The owner explicitly approved removing the historical blanket Sneak Attack exclusions for undead, constructs, and creatures with unusual anatomy. Sneak Attack does not bypass actual damage-type resistance or immunity or a separately defined applicable precision-damage immunity. Apply ordinary damage types and defenses. A creature does not become immune merely for lacking ordinary vital organs, being nonliving, or having immunity to critical hits unless another rule expressly grants that protection.
- **Keep distinct historical and modern defenses under clear names.** **Uncanny Awareness** is the approved native name for historical 3.x Dexterity-defense retention when Flat-Footed or facing an unseen attacker; its later anti-flanking enhancement improves **the same Uncanny Awareness feature** without changing its name to Improved Uncanny Awareness. **Uncanny Dodge** remains the separate modern 5.x Reaction-based incoming-damage reduction. Historical 3.5e levels 4/8 and modern level 5 are source-based working placements, not yet independently finalized cross-edition class progression; retain proper defense interactions and any relevant exceptions.
- **Critical escalation is optional and requires natural die results.** Effects that change, substitute or set a d20 result, including Stroke of Luck's numerical result of 20, do not by that change supply the natural die faces required to initiate or advance an optional critical escalation chain. Preserve normal explicit numeric/test success effects and the separate ordinary critical rules. The shared critical-system specification, not a Rogue-specific exception, governs this distinction.

## Approved: historical trap expertise

**Owner approval (2026-10-23):** Retain both **Trapfinding** and **Trap Sense** as Rogue class features, preserving advanced capabilities without making the ordinary detection and disarming of traps exclusive to Rogues.

- **Trapfinding:** Preserve specialized detection and dismantling of difficult or magical traps and the historical option of identifying a safe bypass for the party without disarming a trap. The 3.5e successful Disable Device check exceeding the trap DC by 10 is the reference for the bypass capability; map Search and Disable Device to native Competencies. Characters without this class feature can still reasonably attempt applicable trap searches/disarming within their actual training and means; do not enforce a blanket Rogue-only rule for high-DC or magical traps. The exact native test/DC mapping and other-source feature permissions remain general subsystem work.
- **Trap Sense:** Preserve progressive, trap-specific defensive bonuses to appropriate saving throws and AC against trap attacks. As a source-based Band 1 working progression, 3.5e provides +1 at Rogue level 3, increasing to +2 at 6, +3 at 9, +4 at 12, +5 at 15 and +6 at 18. The 3.0 ability was attached to historical Uncanny Dodge at levels 11, 14, 17 and 20 and should not create an additional duplicate bonus track when converted. Apply the established native save/defense contribution and stacking rules. Numeric adoption awaits the final class-level advancement table; these are source values, not an independent extra owner ruling.
- Trapfinding and Trap Sense are distinct: one provides trap-handling opportunities and one provides defensive protection.

## Source-comparison method — owner clarification

**Owner clarification (2026-10-23):** Compare **3.0 and 3.5e** starting from the owner's assessment that **3.5e generally improves 3.0**, and restore useful earlier content selectively. **Do not assume the same appraisal applies to 5.x.** The owner generally prefers **2014 5e's mechanics over 2024 5.5e's**, regarding many 2024 revisions as weakened while recognizing useful additions such as Weapon Mastery. For overlapping Rogue mechanics, scrutinize 2024 replacements rather than adopting them by date; recover valuable 2014 features that were removed, such as **Blindsense**, while also considering genuinely useful 2024 additions such as **Cunning Strike** and **Devious Strikes**. Approved Dorks & Dice decisions override either source, and each difference is still assessed individually, not by a universal edition-precedence rule.

**Specific implication for this Rogue review:** The assistant's suggestion to favor **3.0 Crippling Strike's 1 Strength damage** over **3.5e's 2** purely because native Sneak Attack may qualify on multiple hits was premature and is withdrawn. Evaluate 3.5e's improvement first, with any balancing adjustment justified by demonstrated interaction rather than a presumed need to nerf the revised value.

## Rest-based use recovery — approved general rule, pending feature specifics

**Owner clarification (2026-10-23):** Track ordinary class ability charges through **rests**, not calendar days, unless there is a concrete reason for an exception (e.g., an item genuinely tied to a daily cycle or optional survival timekeeping). In this reconciliation, the historical “once per day” **Defensive Roll** allowance should be translated as **one use restored on a Long Rest** under the standard policy. The specific defensive outcome, saving throw, triggering conditions, Reaction interaction and final selectable feature remain **open for review**. No additional Reaction or Short-Rest refresh is implied. Longer source-specific cooldowns already approved elsewhere are not automatically rewritten.

## Approved: Rogue special-ability selections and resolved overlaps (2026-10-23)

**Owner approval:** Retain **four selectable Rogue special-ability grants** at class levels **10, 13, 16 and 19**, using the 3.5e options as the starting point. The following proposals have been approved **except for Defensive Roll's detailed mechanics**, which remain explicitly open. A player makes a choice upon reaching each listed level; the grants are not four automatically acquired special abilities. Choices must be meaningful and do not grant duplicate existing benefits.

| Selection | Approved native reconciliation |
| --- | --- |
| **Crippling Strike** | An eligible Sneak Attack inflicts **2 Strength ability damage** in addition to its normal effects (the 3.5e amount). Each independently qualifying hit can produce the effect; do not introduce a once-per-turn cap merely to offset the approved Sneak Attack frequency. Apply eventual native ability-damage recovery, immunity and stacking rules. |
| **Defensive Roll** | **Partially resolved only.** Retain an emergency-defense option as a candidate for this selection, with **one use restored per Long Rest**, following the approved global resource policy. Its exact trigger, save/DC, damage outcome, Reaction requirement and interaction with Uncanny Dodge are **not approved**. It is not yet an executable class choice. |
| **Improved Evasion** | **Do not offer as a redundant special-ability selection.** Absorb its historical half damage on a *failed* eligible save into the Rogue's ordinary modern-style **Evasion**; the complete modern effect is granted through baseline Evasion, rather than spending a level-up choice on it. This does not eliminate any of the four special-ability grant slots. |
| **Opportunist** | As a selected ability, **once per round** when an ally damages a foe in melee, the Rogue may make the historically granted opportunity attack using the **ordinary shared Reaction**; it never grants a second Reaction. Normal attack/Sneak Attack eligibility still applies. Exact general opportunity-attack semantics remain shared combat-system work. |
| **Skill Mastery** | Replace the historically redundant *take 10 under stress* choice with **Expertise in two additional eligible skills or native competencies**. These must be actual additional Expertise selections, not a second application to a skill that already has Expertise; any repeated selection must provide eligible distinct choices. No new separate fixed numerical modifier is invented. |
| **Slippery Mind** | Retain both **different** benefits: the modern baseline feature at Rogue level **15** grants **Wisdom and Charisma saving throw proficiencies**, while selecting the historical special ability grants **one additional save one round after failing** against a qualifying enchantment or control effect. These are parts of one named feature with a selectable enhancement; baseline proficiency does not require taking the special-ability option. The shared save/timing subsystem handles the retry. |
| **Bonus Feat** | May select **one otherwise eligible feat** instead of another special ability, honoring ordinary prerequisites and the rule that **Epic Boons require total character level 21+**. This grant is separate from, and does not replace or duplicate, scheduled ASI/feat grants. |

**Special-ability choice pool for implementation:** Crippling Strike, Opportunist, Skill Mastery, Slippery Mind's optional retry, and Bonus Feat are approved; the Defensive Roll choice is **reserved and unresolved** until its exact mechanics are approved. Improved Evasion's original benefit is part of baseline Evasion and is **not** a special-ability choice.

## Approved: Rogue modern tactical features and repeated Sneak Attack interaction

**Owner approved the earlier modernization proposals except unresolved Defensive Roll mechanics:**

- **Weapon Mastery (level 1):** Preserve the 2024 selection of two mastered, proficient weapon types and normal source-authorized substitution; Mastery gives its normal effects only, and proficiency—not Mastery—is required for Sneak Attack weapon eligibility.
- **Steady Aim (level 3):** Preserve the 2024 Bonus Action Advantage with its stay-stationary and Speed 0 constraints. **Advantage alone never qualifies** an attack for Sneak Attack under the approved native eligibility rule.
- **Cunning Strike (level 5):** Preserve Poison, Trip and Withdraw as dice-cost options on a qualifying Sneak Attack. The Rogue may use an allowed Cunning Strike on **each independently qualifying Sneak Attack hit**, paying its Sneak Attack damage dice cost **separately for that hit**.
- **Improved Cunning Strike (level 11):** Permit **up to two Cunning Strike effects on one qualifying hit**, paying each required die cost independently. This is a limit *per hit*, not a new turn-level Sneak Attack restriction.
- **Devious Strikes (level 14):** Add the Daze, Knock Out and Obscure choices with their 2024 individual Sneak Attack dice costs. Existing required target saves, equipment prerequisites and effects remain; do not silently turn effects into automatic successes or free riders.
- The shared native damage/conditions/effect engine will adjudicate multi-hit conditions, target saves, poison interactions, and the cost of Sneak Attack dice on critical hits. These are **execution dependencies**, not permission to reconsider the approved per-hit Cunning Strike frequency.

Existing shared foundation rulings, including the critical-escalation option and no unearned class actions, take precedence. **Defensive Roll remains the one special-ability design needing a final decision.**

## Explicitly unresolved: eligibility and related mechanics

The decisions settle **frequency and the high-level eligibility principle**, not every concrete trigger or the executable detection system. The following details remain to be reconciled against 3.0, 3.5e, SRD 5.1, SRD 5.2.1, and established native combat rules:
- Define qualifying detection failures and exploitable vulnerabilities, including the relationship to flanking, denied Dexterity defenses, feints, nearby allies and how Advantage/Disadvantage interacts.
- Remaining precision-damage restrictions (e.g., cover or concealment and explicitly defined precision immunity) under shared detection and damage systems. **Do not reinstate** a 30-foot cap, historical blanket creature-type/anatomy immunity, special Disadvantage for unconventional proficient weapons, or a Finesse/Ranged-only restriction. Weapon Mastery is not a substitute for proficiency.
- Interaction with Cunning Strike's Sneak Attack die costs, ordinary critical damage and optional natural-result escalation, special attack sequences, reaction attacks, and broader action economy.
- **Defensive Roll remains unresolved:** choose its distinct emergency-defense outcome, save/DC and Reaction/same-hit interactions before offering it as a selectable option. Other special-ability selections, modern tactical additions, and the Band 1 advancement direction are approved; trap and detection calculations remain shared-subsystem execution dependencies.
