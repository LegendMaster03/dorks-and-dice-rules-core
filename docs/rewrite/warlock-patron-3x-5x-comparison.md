# Warlock Patron comparison: 3.5e origins versus ten 5.x Patrons

**Status:** research-only cross-edition **reference comparison**, 2026-10-24. Not an approval to include non-SRD Patrons or 3.5e feature trees in the initial SRD-only playable Warlock slice. Existing Patron source conversion, three-pillar structure and prestige-class decisions remain unchanged.

## Counting convention and crucial structural difference

**5.x: ten distinct officially published Patron concepts as of October 2026**: Archfey, Celestial, Fiend, Great Old One, Fathomless, Genie, Hexblade, Undead, Undying, Vestige. There were **nine** distinct previously published official 2014-era concepts; **Vestige**, from *Arcana Unleashed* (released September 15, 2026), is the tenth. These are **concepts**, not a sum of edition/version revisions. Four appear in the 2024 *Player's Handbook* (Archfey, Celestial, Fiend, Great Old One), and Undead received a 2026 *Ravenloft: The Horrors Within* revision. Count Undead and Undying as separate published designs, pending mechanics review; do not claim that they are mechanically the same.

**3.5e Warlock: zero formal 5.x-style Patron subclass selections.** *Complete Arcane* and *Complete Mage* describe supernatural sources and ways to acquire power (including inheritance and actual pacts); `Complete Mage` explicitly discusses fiends, fey, slaadi, and celestials as Warlock origins. The same book has Fey and Fiendish **Heritage feat chains**; they are feats with qualifying prerequisites, **not** 3.5e Warlock subclasses or mandatory Patron selections. Other thematically adjacent 3.5e **base classes**, feats, or prestige specializations may inform individual native parts without being retroactively mislabeled 3.5e Patrons.

This distinction is foundational to direct source-to-native deconstruction: **3.5e features must not be squeezed into fictitious 5e subclasses on import**.

| **Great Old One** | Aberrant/alien and Elder Evil traditions, aberration-oriented source lore and supplemental magic (e.g., *Lords of Madness*) | **Partial thematic analogy; not a directly matching 3.5e Warlock Patron tree** | Separate psychic/telepathic/mental features from alien origin and avoid claiming any specific aberration class was a Great Old One subclass. |

| **Genie** | Genies, elemental outsiders, elemental traditions and genie-themed magic in 3.5e | **Thematic only** | Four elemental Genie subtypes and their variable damage/spells; no evidence of an official 3.5e **Warlock Genie Patron package**. Do not treat every elemental spell as a Patron entitlement. |
| **Hexblade** | 3.5e **Hexblade base class** (*Complete Warrior*) with its curse, martial and arcane features | **Strong mechanical predecessor, but a distinct base class, not a 3.5e Warlock specialization** | Unbundle curse and shadow-themed Patron identity from weapon competence. The enhanced 2024 Pact of the Blade already grants Charisma-based weapon attacks; do not duplicate that entitlement merely to reproduce 2014 Hexblade. |
| **Undead** | Necromancy, undead transformations and related 3.5e class/magic traditions | **Thematic only** | Transformation, fear/necrotic power, undead embodiment; 2026 revised Undead is a separate edition version of the **same** Patron concept, not an eleventh Patron. |
| **Undying** | Lichdom, magical immortality and undead-resistance/longevity traditions in 3.5e | **Thematic only** | Immortality/survival vs Undead's transformation and terror; keep the **two published Patron identities distinct** until individual effects are reconciled, rather than collapsing them on theme alone. |
| **Vestige** | 3.5e **Binder** and vestiges, `Bind Vestige` and `Practiced Binder` feats (*Tome of Magic*) | **Explicit designer-acknowledged conceptual predecessor, but distinct class and gameplay structure** | Bind a remnant power, choose domain/manifestation, companion integration. The new 2026 Vestige Patron grants a **manifest companion** whose features evolve; the original Binder bound vestiges for powers and did **not inherently use the same companion model**. Reuse shared companion system rather than inventing a Patron-only one. |

These analogies are **conversion candidates**, not automatically mechanically equivalent entities. A source may provide provenance for lore, feature designs or a different class without supplying a direct source-to-native feature equivalence.

## What 3.5e contains that the ten Patrons do not directly enumerate

1. **Slaadi/chaotic-source Warlocks** are specifically described as plausible power sources in *Complete Mage*; no identically named published 5.x Patron appears in the ten above.
2. **Power via ancestry** was a recognizable 3.5e Warlock origin rather than necessarily an individual patron contract. Native **Patron** must preserve that possibility at Warlock level 1, as already decided by the owner.
3. **Heritage feat chains** provide granular powers but are general feats rather than class-exclusive subclass feature packages.
4. Historical prestige material such as **Hellfire Warlock** and **Enlightened Spirit** may inform mechanics, but native category remains **case-by-case** for single-class variants. **Eldritch Disciple** and **Eldritch Theurge** involve Warlock plus a different caster base class and therefore must remain **prestige classes** under the approved rule.
5. The original 3.5e **Binder** used its own progression and vestiges. Its adjacency to 2026 Vestige should *not* be used to silently import Binder's mechanics into a Warlock-only Patron part.

## Additional owner direction: future classifications and Patron flexibility (2026-10-24)

**Neither Hexblade nor Vestige has a final native base-class-versus-Warlock ruling.** Their closest 3.5e counterparts, Hexblade and Binder, were distinct **base classes**, not Warlock source prestige classes. Native classification must evaluate whether each has sufficient independent resource/ability progression, a distinct core gameplay role, and separation from Warlock's Patron/Pact/Invocation mechanics to merit a base class, or can best be supported as Warlock-defined parts and a guided Patron preset. Do not merge or split either by default. This is separate from the already approved rules for actual prestige classes.

Warlocks may obtain supernatural power **without a formal Patron contract, without a personally identified external Patron, or without an external Patron entity at all**. The Warlock's **Patron pillar** remains the home of that origin and its class development, not a universal requirement to select a named NPC. Such modes require future native entitlement definitions; their existence does not unlock other Patrons' powers for free.

**Options may have cross-pillar eligibility requirements.** An individual Pact or Invocation could require an eligible Patron trait/part, and a Patron manifestation may depend on a Pact/Invocation. These are explicit typed prerequisites, not globally assumed class restrictions. This principle must be represented in the shared rules contract and must apply equally to guided and custom construction.

**Fiend remains the sole active Patron of the SRD-only vertical slice.** It is particularly well grounded because the 3.5e Warlock had direct fiendish origin/heritage antecedents, and two SRD 5.x variants provide substantive rule differences to reconcile. This study references non-SRD heritage and historical variants without implementing them.

## Implications for designing the native Patron pillar

- Treat **Patron identity/source** and **specific Patron-granted mechanics** separately. A source might be a pact, ancestor, inherited legacy, or other supernatural relationship without mandating a contract.
- A Patron may grant **parts, coherent bundles, upgrade chains, and shared-state choices**. Case examples: Genie has nested elemental specializations; Vestige has subtype/companion choices; Archfey has connected escape/illusion features.
- **Cross-pillar dependencies are necessary:** Hexblade overlaps with Blade Pact; Vestige may coexist with Chain Pact familiars/other companion grants, with costs, stacking, and control handled by established shared companion rules. Do not assume they are identical grants. Patron features may reference invocations but should not be duplicated as invocations merely to maximize modularity.
- Preserve **source provenance separately from the native entitlement**: 2014 versus 2024 versus 2026 subclass revisions, 3.5e heritage feat, 3.5e other base class, or 3.5e prestige class have different acquisition costs and sources.
- Keep a traditional subclass as a **preset** and permit eligible custom combinations via the same native development machinery. Mechanics, class-level acquisition and budgets still require owner-approved reconciliation.
- Preserve **one-SRD-Patron testing limit**: Fiend is the **only** Patron in SRD 5.1/5.2.1; the nine other Patrons are **reference-only during the current vertical slice**. This comparison broadens **design validation**, not the approved playable content inventory.
- References to non-SRD rules should remain **factual or paraphrased design research**; do not embed unlicensed rule text in the SRD-native implementation.

## Questions this comparison answers and leaves open

**Settled:** 3.5e **does not** supply a directly equivalent ten-Patron subclass list; Fiend, Fey and Celestial have comparatively strong Warlock-identity links; Vestige and Hexblade come from distinct historical base classes; none of that requires changing our SRD-only scope.

**Open for later:** Should non-SRD Patrons be integrated in the first implementation? This is a separate owner content-scope decision, **not presumed approved**. Specific cross-Patron ability interchange, eligibility costs, the 2026 subclass update effects, Pact overlap, and non-SRD source mechanics remain to be individually approved.

**Most revealing architecture stress tests without importing non-SRD features:** (1) Patron → Pact overlap (*Hexblade* vs Blade), (2) dynamically selected Patron component (*Genie*), (3) Patron-owned companion (*Vestige*), (4) two superficially similar themes with distinct mechanics (*Undead* vs *Undying*), (5) 3.5e source without a modern counterpart (*Slaadi*).
