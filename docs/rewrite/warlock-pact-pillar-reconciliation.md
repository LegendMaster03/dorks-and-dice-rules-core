# Warlock Pact pillar — approved acquisition architecture

**Status:** Owner-approved level-1 Pact acquisition, Pact-bound Invocation lifecycle, cross-pillar prerequisites, and Patron discretion over accepted agreements/clauses (2026-10-25). Additional Pact acquisition gating and Pact-specific mechanics are deferred. These are design approvals, not activation of the pending native Warlock.

## Three independent pillars

The native Warlock progresses through three distinct pillars:

| Pillar | Conceptual role | Initial native entitlement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Patron** | **Who** the Warlock derives power from | Patron identity at Warlock **level 1** |
| **Pact** | **What form the power relationship takes / terms of the arrangement** | One **dedicated Pact selection at Warlock level 1**, separate from Invocations |
| **Eldritch Invocations** | **Additional clauses/capabilities** that extend or modify the arrangement | Invocation selections remain separately budgeted; preserve level-1 access |

This is a **narrative analogy, not a universal legal-contract requirement**. Warlocks may derive their abilities from inherited Patron power, an unusual arrangement, or setting-specific noncontractual connections. A Pact choice defines the character's magical specialization and its mechanical entitlements regardless of whether a literal bargain was signed. The Patron-pillar rules regarding inherited power and obligations remain in force.

## Approved native Pact rules

1. **Every Warlock receives one first Pact selection at class level 1.** It is a class feature in the **Pact pillar** with its own dedicated selection entitlement.
2. A first Pact **never consumes an Eldritch Invocation selection**. Being a Pact is not equivalent to being an Invocation even though the 2024 source classifies the three as invocations.
3. **Four active Pact reconciliation options:** **Pact of the Blade, Pact of the Chain, Pact of the Tome, Pact of the Talisman**. **Pact of the Star Chain and its Seeker Patron are disabled/deferred together, not deleted**, with their historical UA research retained. Blade/Chain/Tome originate in SRD 5.1/5.2.1; Talisman is a published non-SRD 2014-era Pact Boon from *Tasha's Cauldron of Everything* (and earlier UA); Star Chain is a non-SRD 2016 UA-only option. Star Chain requires the Seeker; the Seeker itself could instead choose Blade, Chain or Tome in the source.
4. Eldritch Invocations represent clauses attached to a specific Pact. They may enhance a Pact or have an explicit Pact prerequisite. These are **additional choices**, using separately approved Invocation entitlements, rather than a second instance of the base Pact grant. Pact affiliation is distinct from a mechanical prerequisite that names Blade, Chain, or Tome.
5. Guided Warlock presets and custom/advanced creation **use precisely the same** first-Pact selection and any additional entitlements. No guided-mode double-grants.
6. Initial Pact and Patron identities both exist at Warlock **level 1**. Any prerequisite depending on one must be evaluated in an order-independent level-up/character-creation process; the player should not be forced to choose one narrative ordering to make an otherwise valid combination.
7. The Pact pillar remains separate from the Patron and Invocation pillars in native data and rules evaluation.

## Pact / Patron / Invocation compatibility and Patron acceptance (owner-approved)

**A Patron determines which Pacts and Invocation clauses it is willing to accept.** This operates alongside explicit game-mechanical restrictions; neither automatically substitutes for the other.

- **Pact requirements:** A Pact definition may restrict eligible **Patron types, traits, features or other Patron qualifications**. Independently, the particular Patron may accept or decline the Pact's terms. Do **not** impose a blanket Patron restriction on Blade, Chain or Tome without one being authored.
- **Invocation requirements:** An Invocation is always attached to a **specific Pact**, even if no special Pact-type prerequisite applies. An Invocation may require a particular **Pact type**, the **Patron associated with that Pact**, **both**, or other features/levels. The associated Patron can also impose terms restricting which additional clauses it will grant.
- **Correct context:** Validate an Invocation against **the Pact to which it is attached and that Pact's Patron**. A different Pact or Patron somewhere else in a future multi-Pact build does not satisfy an unmet restriction.
- **Separate eligibility and consent:** Meeting published prerequisites does **not** automatically establish that a Patron accepts a particular agreement or clause when that Patron has restrictive terms. Conversely, Patron willingness does **not silently waive** explicit prerequisites. A special exception mechanism is not yet approved.
- **Inherited and indirect arrangements:** The relevant Patron's existing or inherited terms can already authorize a Pact or Invocation. Do **not** require live contact, a signed agreement, or fresh personal negotiation whenever an Invocation is selected.
- **Transfer or restoration:** The **receiving Pact** must meet the Invocation's prerequisites, and its Patron must **accept** the restored/transferred clauses under the new or preexisting terms. The prior Patron's permission alone is insufficient.
- **Implementation:** Use **typed, per-option prerequisites** and Pact-to-Patron affiliations, evaluate eligibility and applicable Patron terms equally in guided/custom creation, explain denied choices, and preserve a historical record if a previously acquired Invocation becomes unavailable. Patron identity can be concealed while terms are adjudicated by the setting/DM.

These rules establish **permission and dependency structure**, not automatic restrictions on all Pacts or an unrestricted Patron veto without setting-defined terms. Actual per-option restrictions and adjudication procedures will be decided during each Pact and Invocation reconciliation.

## Pact lifecycle and Pact-bound Invocations (approved design direction)

A **Pact is a major agreement**, not an ordinary Invocation or interchangeable minor customization. **Every Eldritch Invocation is a clause attached to a Pact**. That Pact's continuing existence controls access to its attached Invocations; this is true even for an Invocation that does not require a specific type of Pact.

- **Pact termination:** When a Pact is broken, its attached Invocations **cease to be available**. An Invocation attached to a broken Pact does **not** continue functioning independently.
- **New agreement:** A newly formed Pact **may explicitly restore or transfer** Invocations originating under a previous Pact, if its Patron accepts those clauses, the new terms allow it, and the Invocation's Pact/Patron requirements remain satisfied. **There is no automatic carryover or waiver**.
- **Traceability:** Preserve each Invocation's association with its Pact and its acquisition history, so loss, restoration, or transfer is explainable and auditable. A useful implementation distinction is **unavailable but recorded**, rather than permanently deleting the ability record when its Pact is broken.
- **Multiple Pacts:** The rules/data model must allow *distinct Pact agreements* and therefore identify which Pact provides which Invocation. This does **not** grant additional Pacts in the current default progression.
- **Narrative flexibility:** Pact is an agreement/relationship metaphor, not always a written contract. Established Patron inheritance/noncontractual-source rulings remain valid.

**Not yet specified:** What events constitute a broken Pact, when its benefits stop, whether Invocation selection entitlements are refunded or repurposed, how many Invocations may transfer, transfer costs, whether Invocations may have more than one Pact association, and any additional-Pact gating. Do not invent those mechanics during native encoding. Retaining a historical record does not authorize automatic reacquisition.

## Additional Pacts — advanced possibility, gate deliberately deferred

**Owner direction:** A second or third Pact must remain a possible future *advanced character option*, understood as another **major agreement**. It is **not an ordinary Eldritch Invocation purchase**, and it is neither permanently forbidden nor automatically available under current native progression. Eligibility, earliest level, special entitlements/costs, Pact limits, coexistence rules and the exact terms for Invocation transfer will be decided when implementing advanced options. Current default Warlocks receive **one level-1 Pact**.

## Blade Pact first intrinsic advancement (owner-approved, 2026-10-25)

**Pact of the Blade gains its own second Pact-weapon attack at Warlock class level 5**, whenever the Warlock takes the Attack action with that weapon. The Pact weapon is attacked with **twice total**, rather than once. This does **not** consume an Invocation selection or require the **Thirsting Blade** Invocation.

The historical Thirsting Blade source is preserved, but its existing two-attack benefit is redundant in native rules. The precise treatment of the duplicate Invocation (removal, substitute unique enhancement, or historical conversion shim) remains to be decided. **Devouring Blade** has a published prerequisite on Thirsting Blade which will need to be redirected if retained; it is **not automatically granted**. Do not combine level-5 extra attacks additively with ordinary Extra Attack or unrelated melee class features absent an explicit native rule.

**BG3-based owner approval, all three Pacts:** The level-5 Deepened Pact advancement is approved as an intrinsic, Invocation-free benefit **for Blade, Chain, and Tome independently**. In Blade it provides the already-approved second Pact-weapon attack. In Chain it provides an additional attack when the Pact familiar makes its attacks; exact command/turn integration remains pending. In Tome it grants *Animate Dead*, *Haste*, and *Call Lightning* as Pact spells, each with **one spell-slot-free casting per Long Rest**, independent of normal known-spell and Invocation entitlements; full casting/concentration/creature-resolution integration follows native rules. **This approval does not make weapon bonding universal**: BG3-style binding of ordinary nonmagical weapons is approved specifically for **Pact of the Blade**, which makes the bound weapon magical; Chain and Tome receive their own different benefits. BG3's disarm immunity and its no-throw restriction remain unapproved. Source and native mechanics are distinct. See [Blade source comparison and BG3 review](warlock-pact-blade-source-comparison.md).

## Cross-Patron feature allocation study — research only

A [ten-Patron × three-Pact allocation audit](warlock-patron-pact-feature-allocation-study.md) examines whether parts of published Patron packages can move into **Blade, Chain or Tome**, how to preserve source-specific identity, and how to represent *Patron-origin features expressed through Pacts* without granting duplicate power. The audit's result is that **Hex Warrior's generic weapon competence** is the clearest relocation candidate and is largely already supplied by the native Blade Pact. Other Patrons primarily present signature source powers rather than freely transferable Pact features. Their distinctive effects should ordinarily remain Patron-owned, with possible **same-resource Pact expressions** or optional **Patron-restricted Invocations** considered *case by case*. This study makes **no gameplay grants or removals**, does not reopen approved Fiend decisions, and does not add non-SRD Patrons to the playable slice. Continue to complete the three Pacts first.

## Hexblade pillar-placement question — research, not a decision

The original **3.5e Hexblade** was a distinct base class in *Complete Warrior*. The **2014 Hexblade** in *Xanathar's Guide to Everything* is a **Patron subclass** whose actual power source is a mysterious **Shadowfell force** manifested through sentient magic weapons (possibly related to the Raven Queen, not confirmed). That force may be the Warlock's Patron, while the weapon is the conduit or manifestation. The published Hexblade could choose **Blade, Chain or Tome**, and its curses and Accursed Specter do not necessarily require a Pact weapon.

**Potential design split:** Weapon proficiency, Charisma weapon attacks and ordinary weapon binding fit the **Blade Pact** and are largely native-approved; the power source still belongs to **Patron**; curses/specters/defensive powers could be Patron manifestations or Patron/Pact-restricted Invocations, but their placement remains **unapproved**. A distinct *Hexblade Pact* might duplicate Blade and constrain unrelated choices; consider only when the Fiend+Blade/Chain/Tome vertical slice is playable and tested. No Hexblade playable import or Patron-to-Pact reclassification is authorized.

## Four active Pacts, fifth historical Pact archived — Talisman and Star Chain

[Five-Pact historical source research and current four-Pact scope](warlock-five-pact-reconciliation-scope.md) is authoritative for non-SRD source classification. The earlier expansion is partially superseded: **Talisman remains active for design**, while **Star Chain and Seeker are disabled/archived**. This does not change the **Fiend-only playable Patron catalog**, the SRD import data, the number of initial Pacts, or the acquisition of additional simultaneous Pacts.

- **Talisman:** Official additional 2014-era Pact Boon from *Tasha's Cauldron of Everything* (2020). Baseline **1d4 after failed ability check, proficiency-bonus uses per Long Rest, transferable talisman bearer**, plus 1-hour replacement ceremony. Review **Rebuke, Protection, and Bond of the Talisman** as distinct source-era **optional Invocations**, rather than assuming their effects are freely granted. No unique native base or level-5 upgrade is approved yet.
- **Star Chain — DISABLED ARCHIVE:** WotC **Unearthed Arcana: The Faithful** (2016), **unpublished playtest** Pact Boon. The research records **Augury ritual**, Intelligence-check advantage once per Short/Long Rest, and the 1-hour replacement ceremony. The original source requires a **Seeker** to choose Star Chain **as an alternative** to standard Pact Boons. **Seeker could instead choose Blade, Chain or Tome**; the reverse requirement was never a source rule. Both Seeker and Star Chain are deferred by owner decision, with data preserved and no build availability.
- **Source-era levels:** Both original Pacts were acquired at 2014 Warlock **level 3**; our **already approved separate level-1 first Pact** still controls native class acquisition. Preserve the original levels for provenance.
- **Advance the four active Pacts meaningfully:** Examine **Talisman's** own level-5+ growth without automatically copying **Blade/Chain/Tome's approved BG3-inspired grants** or making related Invocations free. **Do not develop Star Chain or Seeker further in this slice**; preserve their research until explicit reauthorization.
- **Edition classification:** The 3.5e Warlock has **no discrete 5e-style Pact Boons**. The named 4e **Fey/Infernal/Star/etc. Eldritch Pacts** classify power sources, analogous to our **Patron** pillar, not Blade/Chain/Tome/Talisman/Star Chain mechanical Pact selections.

## Source comparisons (preserved, not controlling native costs)

- **2014 / SRD 5.1:** Pact Boons were distinct class choices at Warlock **level 3**.
- **2024 / SRD 5.2.1:** Blade, Chain, and Tome appear among **Eldritch Invocations**, with a level-1 route at an Invocation-selection cost.
- Native Dorks & Dice uses a **dedicated level-1 Pact entitlement** without Invocation cost. This combines an early specialization with a meaningful dedicated Pact identity.
- The two SRD classifications remain unchanged in `data/reference/srd/warlock-srd-slice-inventory.json` (`PB` for 2014, `EI` for 2024). They remain historical records, not alternate native ability definitions.

## Explicitly undecided

- **Additional Pacts:** conceptually permitted as a future advanced option, **not available through current default progression**; their level, cost, maximum, coexistence rules and acquisition restrictions are deferred.
- **Later Pact upgrades/entitlements**: whether a Pact receives any special advancement choices separate from optional Invocations.
- **Mechanics** of the **four active** Pacts individually, including Blade/Eldritch Blast, Chain/familiars, Tome/spells, and Talisman/support. Star Chain/Seeker stays a preserved **disabled research case**; intrinsic level-5 upgrades are approved only for Blade, Chain, and Tome.
- **Invocation budgets, scaling, and individual option-specific restrictions** remain under the Invocations pillar; the typed Patron/Pact eligibility model is approved but does not itself assign restrictions to particular options. Approval of a dedicated first Pact must not rewrite Invocation counts.

## Next case

Additional simultaneous Pacts remain possible but **their gating is intentionally deferred**. Reconcile **Blade, Chain, Tome, and Talisman**; preserve research on **Star Chain and Seeker** in disabled status. The original UA lets Seeker choose normal Pacts, but Star Chain requires Seeker. Neither disabled option should be offered in a build. Pact-termination edge cases and Invocation transfer costs remain follow-up rule design.
